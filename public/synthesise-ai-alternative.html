A Synthesise AI alternative for turning knowledge into products

Synthesise AI helps people package what they know into a digital product. Kitsmith does the same job without a big upfront program fee. This page says what each one does, using only what is publicly stated.

What Synthesise AI does

According to its own site, Synthesise AI helps you find where your skills and your audience's problems overlap, then builds a step-by-step "product charter". A paying user's public tutorial describes the outputs as PDF content and video course outlines with scripts, and says the results need manual polishing and formatting.

That tutorial and the site disagree on how it is sold. The site lists a one-time payment of $2,999. The tutorial says it comes only with Iman Gadzhi's Monetise 2.0 program at $1,995. Check their site for the current terms.

What Kitsmith does

  1. Sweet spot. You describe what you know. It suggests three product ideas, each with an audience, a problem, and a cheap way to test demand before you build.
  2. Blueprint. A step-by-step path from your customer's starting point to a clear result.
  3. Draft. A written guide or a video course with spoken scripts, written one section at a time.
  4. Sales page text. Listing copy for a marketplace such as Gumroad.
  5. Export. Markdown, HTML, or print to PDF.

What neither one does

No tool can promise that a product will sell. The output is a first draft. You add your own real examples, results and proof, then check the facts before you publish. Kitsmith is told not to invent customers, numbers or reviews, and it marks the places where you need to add your own.
