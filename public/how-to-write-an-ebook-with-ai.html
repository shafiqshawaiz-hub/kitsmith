How to write an ebook with AI

AI can draft an ebook quickly, but a draft is not a finished book. The parts that make it worth buying are yours: real examples, correct facts, and a clear result for the reader. Here is an order that works.

1. Decide the reader and the result

Write one sentence: "This ebook helps [specific reader] do [specific result]." If you cannot write it, the topic is too broad.

2. Outline before you draft

Ask the AI for an outline of chapters in the order a beginner needs them. Remove anything that does not serve the result in your sentence. A shorter ebook that finishes the job beats a long one that wanders.

3. Draft one section at a time

Give the AI your outline and ask for one section at a time. Tell it to mark places where your own example belongs instead of inventing one. Long single prompts tend to produce vague text.

4. Add your own examples and check every fact

Replace placeholders with real experience. Check each number, date and claim against a source, or delete it. AI text can sound sure when it is wrong.

5. Edit for voice, then format

Read it aloud and rewrite stiff sentences. Then export to PDF and check headings, page breaks and links on a phone and a laptop.

6. Publish honestly

Describe what the ebook covers and who it is for. Do not promise results. Check the store's rules on AI-assisted content. You can start from a draft made in Kitsmith, which exports Markdown, HTML or print to PDF, and read digital product ideas from your skills if you need a topic.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI write a whole ebook?

It can write a draft of one. A good ebook still needs your examples, fact-checking and editing.

Do I have to disclose AI use?

Some stores require it. Read the rules of where you sell and be honest with readers.

How long should an ebook be?

As long as it takes to deliver the promised result. Shorter and useful is better than long and padded.
