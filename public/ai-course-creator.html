AI course creator: turn what you know into a video course

An AI course creator drafts the plan and the lesson scripts for a video course. It cannot know your real experience, so the useful ones leave room for your examples. This page explains the steps, the limits, and what to check before you sell anything.

What an AI course creator does

It takes a topic and an audience and proposes a course outline, then writes a spoken script for each lesson. Some also write the sales page text. The output is a first draft. Read every script out loud before you record it, because text written to be read often sounds stiff when spoken.

Plan the course around one result

Pick one result a student can reach, such as "build a weekly budget in a spreadsheet". Then list the lessons in the order a beginner needs them. Fewer, shorter lessons are easier to finish and easier to review. Avoid padding the course to look bigger.

Edit the scripts so they sound like you

Replace generic examples with your own. Cut sentences you would not say to a friend. Mark anywhere the AI made a claim you cannot support and either find a source or remove it. Never publish made-up customers, results or testimonials.

Record, then test with one real person

Record screen or voice lessons with a basic microphone. Before you publish, ask one person from your target audience to follow the course and tell you where they got stuck.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI create a video course for me?

It can draft the outline and the scripts. You still record the lessons, add your own examples and check the facts.

Do I need to be on camera?

No. A screen recording with your voice, or slides with a voiceover, works for many topics.

Will a course made with AI sell?

No tool can promise that. Test interest with a free one-page version before you build the whole course.
