Digital product ideas for students

Students already know things other students pay attention to: how to study for a class, how to use a tool, how to get through an application. These eight ideas are starting points, not predictions of what will sell. Check demand before you build.

Eight ideas to start from

  • A study guide for one specific course, written in your own words.
  • A spreadsheet template for budgeting or tracking assignments.
  • A checklist for a process you have done, such as applying for internships.
  • A resume or cover letter template pack with notes on when to use each.
  • A short guide to a tool you use well, such as a note-taking app or Excel.
  • A drill planner or training plan for a sport you coach or play.
  • A practice question set for a class, with worked answers you checked.
  • A step-by-step guide to a hobby skill, such as editing short videos.

Pick one using three checks

Choose the idea where you can answer yes to all three: people ask you about it, you can show a real result from your own use, and you can describe the buyer in one sentence. If one answer is no, pick another idea.

Respect your school's rules

Do not sell or share material your school or publisher owns, such as copyrighted textbook content, exam questions or lecture slides. Write from your own understanding, and check your institution's policy before selling anything related to coursework.

Test before you build

Make a free one-page version and share it in a class group chat or forum. Count who asks for more. If nobody does, change the topic or the audience.

Frequently asked questions

Can a student sell digital products?

Yes, as long as the content is your own and you follow your school's and the store's rules. Check both before you publish.

What is the easiest digital product to make?

A short checklist or template, because it is small and quick to test.

How much will I earn?

Nobody can say. Results depend on the topic, the audience and the work you put in.
