Digital product ideas from your skills

The best first digital product usually comes from something you already do and people already ask you about. Here are five questions that turn your skills into a specific idea, the formats that fit each answer, and a quick way to check demand before you build.

Five questions to find your idea

  1. What do people ask you for help with? Repeated questions are a signal of demand. Write down the last five.
  2. What did you learn the hard way? A mistake you fixed is a product: "how I fixed X" saves someone else the time.
  3. What do you do that others find hard? If it feels easy to you, you may be skipping steps that a beginner needs written down.
  4. Who is one specific person this helps? "Home bakers with dense loaves" is a better audience than "people who like food".
  5. What result can they hold at the end? A filled-in sheet, a working template, one finished task.

Formats that fit each kind of skill

  • You explain things well: a short step-by-step guide (PDF).
  • You build or organise things: a template, tracker or spreadsheet.
  • You do the same task every week: a checklist pack.
  • You show things best out loud: a short video course with scripts.

Examples (illustrative, not results)

A student who tutors first-year economics might write a "how to read a supply and demand question" guide. Someone who coaches youth soccer might make a drill planner for 8 to 10 year olds. A home baker might write a one-week plan to fix dense sourdough. These are ideas to show the pattern. They are not claims about what any product earns.

Check demand before you build

Make a free one-page version of the idea and offer it where your audience already is: a class group chat, a forum, a social post. Count the replies and write down the questions people ask. If nobody responds, adjust the topic or the audience before you write the full product.

Frequently asked questions

What digital product can I make from my skills?

Match what you know to a format: a step-by-step guide, a template or spreadsheet, a checklist pack, or a short video course. Pick a narrow topic where you can show a real result.

How do I know if anyone wants my idea?

Offer a free one-page version where your audience already spends time and count how many people ask for more before you build the full product.
